I am working on a hackathon project called:

AI for Individualised Mastery Support (AIMS)

The wider problem is that lecturers teaching large classes struggle to provide timely and personalised feedback for open-ended assignments. Students often receive only a mark or generic feedback, making it difficult for them to understand their mistakes and identify what they should practise next.

For the hackathon prototype, I want to narrow the scope to AI-assisted marking of handwritten mathematics homework.

The intended workflow is:

1. A lecturer provides a mathematics question.
2. The lecturer provides a model solution and marking rubric.
3. The lecturer uploads a student’s handwritten solution as an image or scanned document.
4. The system recognises the handwritten mathematics and converts it into editable text or LaTeX.
5. The system analyses the student’s working steps, not only the final answer.
6. It compares the student’s solution with the model answer, rubric and relevant learning materials.
7. It recommends marks for each step or rubric criterion.
8. It identifies mathematical errors and possible misconceptions.
9. It generates personalised feedback explaining what the student did correctly, what went wrong and how to improve.
10. It generates targeted practice questions based on the student’s weaknesses.
11. The lecturer reviews and can edit or override all AI-generated marks and feedback.

I am currently considering the following technologies and ideas:

- OCR or mathematical handwriting recognition
- Image preprocessing
- LaTeX extraction and editing
- Multimodal language models
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation using rubrics, model solutions, lecture notes and worked examples
- Symbolic mathematics tools for checking whether equations and answers are mathematically equivalent
- Misconception detection
- AI-generated targeted practice questions

However, I do not want you to assume that all of these are necessary or that my current approach is the best one. Critically evaluate the idea and recommend the most practical solution.

This is a hackathon project built by a team of around four to five students. The prototype must be achievable within a limited development period. We do not need a production-ready platform or support for every type of mathematics.

The MVP should focus on one manageable category of handwritten mathematics, such as algebra, quadratic equations, differentiation or another topic you believe is more suitable.

Important considerations:

- Mathematical handwriting recognition may be inaccurate.
- Users should be able to correct the extracted LaTeX before marking.
- The system should assess methods and intermediate working, not just final answers.
- The system should provide marking recommendations rather than make irreversible grading decisions.
- The output should be explainable and connected to the rubric.
- The demonstration must still work even if OCR fails.
- We should avoid training a handwriting model from scratch unless there is a very strong reason.
- The system should avoid exposing student personal data or API keys.
- The final prototype should have a clear and impressive end-to-end demonstration.
